# Voice Guide: Home Coffee Brewing Basics

## Audience

Home cooks and morning-coffee people who want practical knowledge without
coffee-snob tone. Curious, time-pressed, and a little embarrassed they
do not know what a burr grinder is.

## Voice

- Short sentences. Direct. Second person.
- Bottom-line first, then the why.
- Plain language. If a term is jargon (extraction, bloom), define it the
  first time.
- Confident without lecturing. The student is an adult who can pick up
  one new habit at a time.

## Reading level

Grade 8. Reach for the everyday word over the precise one.

## Things never to do

- No em dashes. Use periods or commas.
- No "of course," "obviously," or "simply." Anything obvious does not
  need saying.
- No coffee-snob signaling. The student does not care about your
  certifications.
- No metric/imperial purism. Both work. Pick one per lesson and stay
  consistent.

## Diagram conventions

- Node labels are short, second person where it fits ("Pick pour-over"
  not "The user selects pour-over"). No em dashes inside labels.
- One sentence of prose ending in a colon introduces every diagram.
- Diagrams sit between two paragraphs of prose, never as the first or
  last thing in a section.

## Sample passage

> Pour-over takes four minutes and one piece of gear: a dripper. The
> ritual is short, the result is clean, and you can do it half awake.
> Drip is faster on the clock but the machine cleans itself slowly. Pick
> by what your morning has, not by what makes you sound serious.
